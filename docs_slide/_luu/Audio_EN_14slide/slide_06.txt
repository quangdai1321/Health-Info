One analyte can have many names.
For white blood cells, we see W B C, S L B C, the full Vietnamese name, and a version with a star.
The percent and hash markers are also important. They separate a percentage from an absolute count.
And some laboratories write them before the name, some after.
But units are the serious problem. Here is a real example.
The report says haemoglobin is fourteen point five six grams per decilitre.
Our reference interval is one hundred and thirty, to one hundred and seventy-five grams per litre.
Without conversion, the system says seek care. That is a false alarm, at the most serious level.
With conversion, the value is one hundred and forty-five point six, and the result is normal.
So our rule is strict. If the unit is unknown, or missing, we refuse the analyte. We never guess.
This is a known problem. Six to nineteen percent of tests cannot be mapped accurately to LOINC.
And one analysis found two thousand and nineteen different unit expressions.
